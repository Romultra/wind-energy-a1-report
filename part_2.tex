The measurements are available at specific heights, including 57 m and 70 m for wind speed, and 41 m for wind direction. For the long-term climate analysis and the Annual Energy Production (AEP) calculation of the V52 turbine, the data collected at the 70 m height is of primary interest.

\subsection{Raw Data and Gaps}

\subsubsection{Data Cleaning}
Before plotting, outliers were removed from the dataset. All wind speed measurements exceeding 37~m/s or below 0 were replaced with NaN, as these are physically unreasonable for Denmark, removing 888 values across all wind speed channels. Additionally, any sensor channel reporting the exact same value for six or more consecutive 10-minute records was flagged as a stuck sensor and replaced with NaN. In total, 128,067 values were removed across all channels, the majority from stuck direction sensors.

\subsubsection{Data Gap Analysis}
Gaps were identified as any interval between consecutive timestamps exceeding the expected 10-minute resolution. A total of 950 timestamp gaps were found, ranging from 11~minutes to approximately 45~hours. Only 12 gaps exceeded 3~hours.

In addition to timestamp gaps, individual sensor channels exhibit their own periods of missing data. Figure~\ref{fig:q10_gaps} shows the per-channel data availability. The most significant outages are the sonic anemometer at 57~m (17.9\% missing, with a prolonged outage in 2019) and the cup anemometer at 70~m (14.8\% missing, with degraded availability in late 2024--2025). Note that the 57~m sonic outage affects both wind speed (SWsp\_57m) and direction (Sdir\_57m) equally, as both quantities are derived from the same instrument. The remaining channels have 7--8\% missing data.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/Q10_Global_Gaps.png}
    \caption{Data availability per sensor channel. Red segments indicate missing data periods longer than 1~hour.}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/Q10_Gap_Histogram.png}
    \caption{Distribution of timestamp gap durations on a log-log scale.}
    \label{fig:q10_hist}
\end{figure}

The gap duration histogram (Figure~\ref{fig:q10_hist}) reveals two distinct regimes. The vast majority of gaps are short (10--30~minutes), consistent with transient telemetry errors or brief signal loss during wireless data transmission. A smaller population of gaps spans hours to days, pointing to more sustained causes.

Possible reasons for the observed gaps include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Telemetry errors:} The high frequency of short gaps (10--20~min) suggests intermittent signal loss in the wireless data link between the mast and the logging server.
    \item \textbf{Instrument failure:} The extended sonic outage at 57~m in 2019 and the cup anemometer degradation at 70~m in late 2024--2025 indicate localized hardware failures specific to individual sensors.
    \item \textbf{Scheduled maintenance:} Simultaneous gaps across multiple channels are consistent with planned inspections and sensor recalibration.
\end{itemize}
